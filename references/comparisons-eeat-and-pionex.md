# Comparisons, E-E-A-T, and Pionex Guidance

Use this reference for product comparisons, commercial topics, financial calculations, and Pionex articles written in the Semrush-style format.

## Source Order

Use the most direct authoritative evidence available:

1. Current official product documentation, support center, terms, or fee schedule
2. Current official product page or announcement
3. Original regulatory text, research paper, or dataset
4. Reputable independent analysis when primary evidence does not answer the question

Do not use a search snippet as the final evidence when the underlying page is available. If an official product page and a detailed fee schedule conflict, prefer the more specific, recently updated schedule and note the discrepancy.

## Claim Ledger

For changing comparisons, track these fields while researching:

| Claim | Value | Conditions | Source | Checked date |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Conditions should capture phrases such as:

- Up to
- Variable
- Limited time
- Requires a paid tier
- Requires a minimum balance
- Applies only to eligible purchases
- Depends on region or account

The published article does not need to expose the internal ledger, but its tables and prose must preserve the conditions.

## Normalize the Comparison

Compare the same economic layers for every product:

1. What the user deposits or holds
2. Who controls the funds
3. What activity creates the yield or reward
4. What happens during a purchase
5. Which fees apply
6. Which conditions unlock the advertised rate
7. What asset the reward is paid in
8. What can cause loss, restriction, or liquidation
9. Where and for whom the product is available

Do not compare one provider's maximum promotional rate with another provider's base rate without labeling both.

## Common Calculations

### Yield

Approximate annual yield:

`average eligible balance × annual rate`

Approximate monthly yield for a simple illustration:

`average eligible balance × annual rate ÷ 12`

### Cashback

`eligible spending × applicable cashback rate`

For progressive cashback, calculate each band separately and sum the results.

### Net Value

`yield + cashback + other measurable rewards − transaction fees − subscriptions − conversion costs − borrowing interest − other modeled costs`

### Reward Break-Even

Spending required for cashback to equal yield:

`average balance × APY ÷ cashback rate`

Spending required to justify a paid tier:

`annual subscription fee ÷ incremental cashback rate`

Use the difference between the paid and free reward rates, not the paid rate alone.

## Honest Experience Signals

Use first-hand evidence only when it exists. Suitable evidence can include:

- Dated product screenshots captured during the task
- A documented application or transaction test
- Original analytics or survey data
- A named expert review actually completed

Otherwise state “documentation-based comparison” or equivalent. Recommend a hands-on test as a publication improvement rather than pretending one occurred.

## Pionex Application

When writing for Pionex:

- Review the current relevant Help Center or Intercom collection first.
- Check existing Pionex blog coverage and avoid creating a page with the same primary intent.
- Use existing product guides as internal links, not substitutes for current official verification.
- If the article compares Pionex with competitors, disclose Pionex's relationship near the top.
- State provider figures as “Pionex publishes,” “the fee schedule lists,” or similar language when the claim has not been independently tested.
- Do not generalize Visa terms to Mastercard, DogPay, or another card program.
- Distinguish card-account balances from primary-account balances.
- Distinguish base rates from boosted tiers, promotions, or partner rewards.
- Explain whether cashback actually offsets transaction fees for the specific transaction type.
- Treat eligibility shown in a user's verified account as more specific than a broad marketing statement.

## Visual Evidence for Product Articles

Prefer:

- An annotated screenshot of the exact rate or fee claim
- A flow diagram showing where funds move
- A normalized feature table with an “as of” date
- A chart showing net value after fees
- A risk diagram linking each risk to the relevant product layer

Avoid:

- Decorative coins and payment cards with no informational role
- Screenshots of old promotional rates without capture dates
- Charts whose axes or assumptions are missing
- Competitor logos used as visual endorsements
- A hero image containing a temporary rate that will quickly become stale
