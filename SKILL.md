# Semrush Editorial Writing

Create an article that helps a reader make sense of a topic, not a keyword-shaped product page. Match the reference articles' editorial architecture without copying their wording or imitating an individual author's voice.

Read [references/semrush-article-format-and-eeat-brief.md](references/semrush-article-format-and-eeat-brief.md) before drafting. It contains the research basis and detailed editorial brief for this skill.

## Choose the Right Workflow

- Use this skill when the user requests a Semrush-style format or a rich, evidence-led guide.
- For Pionex articles, this skill governs the research, structure, article body, E-E-A-T, and visual plan.
- Do not automatically add SEO metadata, JSON-LD, social posts, or a Pionex product-post template. Add those only when requested.
- If the user also requests a full Pionex production package, write the article with this skill first, then use the relevant Pionex packaging instructions without replacing this article structure.

## Before Writing

1. Read every reference article or source the user supplied.
2. Identify the target question, reader, and search intent.
3. Check existing related coverage before proposing a new page. Give each article a distinct job and avoid repeating a parent guide or product review.
4. For a topic cluster, decide whether the draft is a parent page or a supporting page. A support article should go deeper on one branch instead of summarizing the whole parent again.
5. Verify time-sensitive facts against current primary sources. Prefer official product documentation, support pages, terms, regulatory sources, and original datasets.

If the requested angle is clear, proceed without asking the user to approve an outline.

## Build the Article

Use this default progression, adapting it to the topic:

1. A clear, benefit-led title.
2. Byline, fact-check or update date, and estimated reading time when appropriate.
3. A short introduction that answers the main question immediately and explains the important distinction.
4. A table of contents for long guides.
5. Definition or context needed to understand the topic.
6. A framework, process, or comparison that answers the core intent.
7. Real examples, calculations, screenshots, or source-backed data.
8. Costs, limitations, mistakes, or risks.
9. A practical checklist or decision framework.
10. A concise next step with natural internal links.
11. Editorial methodology and primary sources.
12. Separate production notes for images, charts, and data.

Headings should answer recognizable reader questions. Make each major section understandable on its own for readers arriving from search or an AI answer.

Read [references/editorial-format.md](references/editorial-format.md) when drafting or revising the article body.

## Apply E-E-A-T Honestly

- Do not invent testing, personal experience, expert reviewers, credentials, quotes, or survey results.
- State whether the work is based on documentation, hands-on testing, original data, or another method.
- Add a visible “checked” or “last verified” date for changing product terms.
- Attribute claims near the relevant text and include direct links to primary sources.
- Distinguish fixed, variable, promotional, conditional, estimated, and “up to” figures.
- State material commercial relationships. When writing for Pionex, disclose that Pionex publishes the guide and offers one of the products discussed.
- If evidence conflicts, use the most specific and recently updated official source and explain the difference when it affects the reader.
- Treat provider marketing claims as provider claims, not independently proven outcomes.

## Comparisons and Calculations

- Compare products using the same decision fields.
- Do not call a product “best,” “safest,” or “highest” without current, stated criteria and a sufficiently complete comparison set.
- Put an “as of” date near rate, fee, availability, or feature tables.
- Show the formula and assumptions for every meaningful calculation.
- Calculate net value after fees when the article discusses yield, cashback, subscriptions, borrowing, conversion, or rewards.
- Separate APY, APR, cashback, rebates, bonuses, and partner offers. Never add percentages that apply to different bases.
- Label hypothetical scenarios as illustrations, not forecasts.

Read [references/comparisons-eeat-and-pionex.md](references/comparisons-eeat-and-pionex.md) for detailed comparison and Pionex-specific guidance.

## Visual and Data Plan

Recommend visuals only when they explain, compare, or substantiate something. Do not fill the article with decorative stock images.

For each recommended asset, specify:

- Exact placement
- Asset type
- Information it must show
- Why it helps the reader
- Source or calculation basis
- Required disclaimer or capture date

Useful assets include process diagrams, decision trees, normalized comparison tables, annotated product screenshots, worked examples, and charts derived from stated data.

When a chart is recommended, provide the exact chart-ready values and label assumptions. Keep production notes separate from the publishable article.

## Pionex Content Boundaries

- Check existing Pionex Card or product articles and link to them for deeper product detail instead of duplicating them.
- Use current Pionex Help Center or Intercom documentation for fees, rates, eligibility, card networks, supported assets, and regional claims.
- Keep nationality, residence, account eligibility, merchant acceptance, and local law as separate concepts.
- Keep yield, cashback, partner rewards, transaction fees, and borrowing costs separate.
- If Pionex is one of the compared providers, use the same level of scrutiny and table fields applied to competitors.
- Do not force a Pionex CTA into every section. One natural next step is usually enough.

## Final Quality Check

Before delivering, confirm:

- The opening answers the title's question.
- The article has a distinct job within the topic cluster.
- Important claims are current and source-backed.
- Tables compare like with like.
- Calculations reconcile.
- Risks and limitations are easy to find.
- No experience or expertise was invented.
- Visual recommendations contain actionable production details.
- Production notes are clearly separated from article copy.
- No unrelated SEO or schema package was added unless requested.
